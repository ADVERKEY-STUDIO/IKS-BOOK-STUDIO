COUNT AND SCALE
The source has 55 PDF containers, not 55 physical interior pages. Printed
folios run 1–97. Illustrated entries occupy printed pages 8–89. Do not copy
this count to another title: count its actual opening, main and closing text.
Single reference page: 630 × 630 points = 222.25 × 222.25mm. Spread: 1260 ×
630 points = 444.5 × 222.25mm. Web Book Studio uses a proportional 210mm
square / 420 × 210mm spread. Keep one consistent scale in the new book.
Body around 15.75pt, original verses 19.5pt, labels 12pt in the reference.
The reviewed English prose uses Charis SIL; original lettering includes
outlined fonts. Match visual hierarchy and readable proportions, and report
font substitutions rather than pretending exact matching.

COVER OBSERVATIONS
The deep-red 'Durga Chalisa' title occupies the top roughly 16% of the square.
'for Young Minds' is a smaller bold dark-teal line below; a third, smaller
serif line reads 'With Meanings and Life Lessons for Children'. The title
area is pale cream, lightly textured. Gold flourishes flank the middle line.
Durga dominates the lower left/centre; a golden circular halo sits behind her
crown. A girl in teal and a boy in yellow look towards her warmly. Broad dark
teal foliage frames the sides; white flowers and a pink lotus add selective
colour. This is an intimate, welcoming scene, not a distant shrine portrait.
New covers should preserve this relationship and hierarchy while using the
new deity's correct identity and source-appropriate objects. Keep lettering
editable. Do not paste this Durga cover into another book.

INTERIOR VISUAL SYSTEM
Warm textured gouache, broad matte shapes, cut-paper-like layering, delicate
fabric patterns, rounded expressive faces and large dark eyes. Restrained
modelling, warm cheeks, black/dark flowing hair, clear silhouettes and gold
ornament. No glossy plastic shading, 3D realism or cinematic lighting.
Teal/navy foreground foliage, dusty blue distant hills, terracotta buildings,
ochre stone, gold light, pink lotus accents and pale warm sky recur throughout.
The setting changes with the verse: temple steps, mountains, rivers, forest,
home, symbolic landscape or battlefield. Repeated colours do not mean
repeating the same scene. Both left-cloud/right-scene and right-cloud/left-
scene arrangements occur. The cloud has overlapping soft scalloped lobes,
subtle transparency at its edge and a quiet light centre.
All prose sits within ONE reading area. Keep room for five text blocks, not
only the original verse. The reading area must not obscure limbs or faces.

IDENTITY AND SMALL-DETAIL LESSONS FROM THE REVIEW
Keep the principal Durga face, crown, sari, bindi, skin tone and hair stable.
Other named forms have distinct attributes; do not give them identical
outfits or eight arms by default. A visible missing arm differs from a limb
hidden naturally by the body. Trace each arm from shoulder through elbow,
wrist and hand. Check hand-to-object contact, finger-supported chakras,
continuous trident shafts, feet and visible toes, ears/earrings, tilak/bindi,
lion paws, veena construction and all small background people.
The review repaired inconsistent Prahlad clothing, Shankaracharya robe/hood
and forehead marks, missing hands, obsolete overlays hiding corrected
fingers, a standing army inconsistent with the aftermath text, and false
lettering on book spines. These are checks to repeat in every new edition.
Anatomy and typography must be assessed again AFTER the cloud is placed.

EDITORIAL SYSTEM
The original traditional verse is retained. Hindi meaning, English meaning,
Hindi moral and English moral follow in clearly labelled separate blocks.
Meanings explain both halves accurately; morals are interpretations for
everyday life, not literal translations. The two languages agree in content.
The tone is respectful and clear, for approximately ages 9–14 and families.
Use a specific connection: deity's action/quality -> practical child action.
Examples in editorial-examples.json show comforting someone who is sad,
meeting change patiently, facing fear with a slow breath/trusted adult,
self-discipline and resisting endless wants. Do not reuse them where the
current verse does not support them.
The review corrected 'mouth' versus 'face', wrong Hindi agreement, heaven
versus a mistranslated realm, an invented penance called 'Aacharaj', difficult
adult wording and disconnected morals. Faithfulness comes before word count.
Preserve devotional language while distinguishing prayers and assurances
from factual guarantees. Source variants remain explicit editorial questions.

GLOSSARY AND END MATTER
Cream single pages, large brown serif 'Glossary', smaller subtitle, red Hindi
terms, a simple Hindi definition then matching English, muted references.
Words follow first appearance. Recalculate all references after pagination.
Do not call the closing prayer 'Chaupai 41' just because it is entry 41.
The back cover echoes the front's cream/teal/pink botany with more quiet space.
Its description accurately promises illustrated verses, meanings and lessons.
Only use publisher data actually supplied for the new edition. ISBN is not
invented or copied across books. No hidden-object game in this series.

REFERENCE LIMITATIONS NOT TO COPY
The original/final PDF contains some approximately 101ppi spread art and
143ppi cover art. These reading references cannot supply true 300ppi detail.
Generate or obtain adequate source-resolution art; merely enlarging pixels
does not recover detail. Check the new rendered proof before publication.
The reference's Hiranyaksha/Hiranyakashipu variant is a recorded publisher
decision, not permission to propagate a mistaken name to another source.
