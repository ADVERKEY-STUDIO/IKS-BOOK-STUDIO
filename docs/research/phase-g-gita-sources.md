# Phase G: Bhagavad Gita pilot sources

Researched 12 September 2026. This is a source-verification and editorial draft, not publication approval. No assets were downloaded and no paid generation was used for this research.

## Sanskrit source and scope

The text witness is IIT Kanpur's **Gita Supersite**, its displayed **मूल श्लोकः** for each selected verse. This identifies an institutional digital transcription; it does not establish a particular printed critical edition or claim collation against manuscripts. Modern translations and commentaries on the same pages are separate works and are not copied into the pilot. Preserve the following original Sanskrit independently of the newly drafted English meanings.

### Bhagavad Gita 2.47 — action

```text
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि।।2.47।।
```

Verified against [IIT Kanpur, 2.47](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&ecsiva=1&etassa=1&etradi=1&field_chapter_value=2&field_nsutra_value=47&htshg=1&language=dv&setgb=1).

**Original English paraphrase — draft:** Give your care to the work before you. Do not make its reward your reason for acting, and do not turn away from action.

Editorial caveat: this is a short reading aid, not an exhaustive translation of *adhikāra* or *karma-phala-hetu*. Do not turn the verse into a promise of success or an instruction never to plan. Keep the final prohibition against attachment to inaction.

### Bhagavad Gita 6.19 — steadiness

```text
यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता।
योगिनो यतचित्तस्य युञ्जतो योगमात्मनः।।6.19।।
```

Verified against [IIT Kanpur, 6.19](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&field_chapter_value=6&field_nsutra_value=19&language=dv&scang=1).

**Original English paraphrase — draft:** A flame sheltered from the wind stays steady. It offers an image of the disciplined mind, gathered in meditation on the Self.

Editorial caveat: preserve the meditation/Self context; this is more than a general productivity slogan. A museum lamp can support the metaphor visually but must not be described as the historical lamp referred to by the verse.

### Bhagavad Gita 11.12 — radiance

```text
दिवि सूर्यसहस्रस्य भवेद्युगपदुत्थिता।
यदि भाः सदृशी सा स्याद्भासस्तस्य महात्मनः।।11.12।।
```

Verified against [IIT Kanpur, 11.12](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&etadi=1&etradi=1&field_chapter_value=11&field_nsutra_value=12&language=dv&scram=1&scsh=1).

**Original English paraphrase — draft:** Imagine a thousand suns rising together. Their light might suggest the radiance of the divine form revealed to Arjuna.

Editorial caveat: retain the comparison as a comparison. A landscape or different iconographic form used alongside this verse is an editorial association, not documentary evidence of the precise scene.

### Typesetting and review

- These transcriptions retain the source's joined sandhi, avagraha, and numeral convention. Store the displayed strings before any presentation-only number styling; do not silently replace सङ्ग with संग or split compounds.
- The digital source uses doubled danda characters `।।` around the verse number rather than the single Unicode double danda `॥`. Any normalization should be explicit and reviewable.
- The three passages are selections from different chapters, not consecutive verses or a complete edition. Label the pilot **Selected verses from the Bhagavad Gita**.
- The Bhagavad Gita should not be labelled a Vedic samhita. The pilot is within the user's broader devotional/classical-text scope.
- English text here was newly drafted for this pilot. A Sanskrit/editorial reviewer should assess its adequacy before publication; this document records no such approval.

## Museum artwork candidates

The following official Met object pages explicitly display **Public Domain**. The first-party API returned `isPublicDomain: true` and the `primaryImage` URL listed for each. The object pages also describe unrestricted commercial and noncommercial use of their open-access public-domain images. These findings apply to the identified images, not every image on the museum website. Keep museum credit and label cropped details.

### 1. Quiet detail: Element of a Lamp

- Object: **21.153**, attributed to India, 18th–19th century, bronze. Credit: **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Gift of Joseph Brummer, 1921**.
- [Official object and rights page](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/447421)
- [Official API record](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/447421)
- [Official full image](https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/is/original/sf21-153b.jpg)
- Fit: an object study next to 6.19; preserve its identification as a lamp element. Do not invent a lit flame, historical provenance in the Gita, or specifically Hindu ritual attribution: the catalogue places it in Islamic Art and says only attributed to India.

### 2. Indian painted manuscript: Razmnama folio

- Object: **55.121.33**, *The White Horse Got Stuck to a Rock in Mount Vindhyachal*, 1616–17, attributed to India; ink, opaque watercolor, gold. Credit: **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Rogers Fund, 1955**.
- [Official object and rights page](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/451317)
- [Official API record](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/451317)
- [Official full image](https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/is/original/sf55-121-33a.jpg)
- Fit: character/landscape detail and complete manuscript example. The museum identifies Arjuna, his brothers and Krishna rediscovering a sacrificial horse. This is a Persian Mahabharata narrative folio; it is **not** the Gita teaching scene. Preserve that distinction in any caption, and do not present its Persian text as Sanskrit.

### 3. Universal-form reference: Navagunjara

- Object: **2006.240**, *Navagunjara, a Universal Form of Krishna*, ca.1835, India (Rajasthan, Jodhpur[?]); opaque watercolor, ink, gold. Credit: **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Purchase, Evelyn Kranes Kossak Gift, 2006**.
- [Official object and rights page](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/73296)
- [Official API record](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/73296)
- [Official full image](https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/as/original/DP140154.jpg)
- Fit: a distinct historic visualization of Krishna's universal form. The museum describes nine animal parts and an encounter with Arjuna. Do not equate this composite iconography with a literal illustration of 11.12. It is better treated as a labelled comparative art reference than as a seamless continuation of another artist's Krishna character.

### Additional landscape candidate from Nepal

Object **2019.65**, *Folio from a Bhagavata Purana series*, ca.1775–1800, Nepal, watercolor/ink. The museum discusses the series' relationship to Indian court painting and describes a Krishna–Arjuna carriage/forest narrative. Credit: **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Purchase, Friends of Asian Art Gifts, 2019**. Public-domain status and API image verified. [Object page](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/819647), [API](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/819647), [full image](https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/as/original/DP-18169-001.jpg). Visually inspect the selected folio before making character-specific crops because the catalogue narrative discusses two folios. A whole painting plus credited detail could support a coherent pilot; Nepal must remain correctly credited and the scene must not be renamed the Gita dialogue.

## Production implication

Historic art can support an honestly labelled, carefully typeset **heritage-art pilot** without paid generation. It cannot by itself demonstrate newly commissioned, continuous character acting across three original scenes. Mixed artists and distinct narrative episodes must not be disguised as one continuous story. Record this limitation when evaluating the pilot's visual and workflow quality.
