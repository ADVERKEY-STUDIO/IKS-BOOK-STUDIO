## First visual observations

### Sita’s Ramayana — strong illustrated-narrative candidate

Tara Books, Samhita Arni and Moyna Chitrakar; designer Jonathan Yamakami. ISBN 9789380340036, 156 pages. [Publisher and interior gallery](https://tarabooks.com/shop/sitas-ramayana/).

Inspected publisher gallery images 2 and 3 in the browser. The first places a compact text column beside a dominant portrait of three figures and vegetation. The second changes to a broad action scene and smaller sequential panels, with white caption areas interrupting saturated artwork. Strong outlines and character treatment connect the samples despite different compositions. This is a promising example of coherent variation; small preview text was not checked for print legibility. Modern retelling, not an original Sanskrit scripture edition.

Observed image sources: [sample 1](https://tarabooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/sitas_ramayana_1.jpg), [sample 2](https://tarabooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/sitas_ramayana_2.jpg). Links are evidence, not permission to host these images. The publisher’s separate Explore PDF is a teaching guide, not an interior scan.

### Bhagavad Gita: The Song of God — scripture typography candidate

Mandala Publishing, 15 February 2022, ISBN 9781647226787, 768 pages. [Official distributor and LOOK gallery](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Bhagavad-Gita/Swami-B-P-Puri/9781647226787).

Browser inspection confirmed a real interior title spread after the cover: generous white space, restrained centered serif title on the right, a much smaller credit block, and a largely empty left page. The richly ornamented cover does not represent the treatment of this interior. Also inspected the copyright/contents spread and the continuation of the contents: clear serif chapter listings, transliterated Sanskrit titles, English subtitles, dotted leaders and broad margins. Ordinary verse and commentary pages require separate assessment; the title spread alone is insufficient for approval.

### Bhagavadgita, Ms. Coll. 390 Item 551 — historical typography reference

[University of Pennsylvania OPenn record](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/html/mscoll390_item551.html), Sanskrit, 45 leaves. The catalogue describes red emphasis and ruled margins. Inspected [fol. 1v–2r image](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/mscoll390_item551/data/web/5791_0001_web.jpg): compact handwritten text blocks on warm paper with broad outer margins; two leaves are photographed vertically. This is a manuscript reference, not a modern bound facing-page design and not a polished illustrated-gallery hero. Use for historical text-block study rather than imitating deterioration. The item record explicitly marks its images/content public domain and metadata CC BY 4.0; retain attribution and item identity.

Keep Ganesha titles and other children’s books in a separate filter, rather than letting them define the gallery’s default adult sacred-book identity. Historical manuscripts should also have a distinct label.

## Selection rules for the next pass

- Require three representative interiors per proposed card: opening, ordinary text, and illustrated or dense page. Record exact sample links or page numbers.
- Assess text hierarchy, composition, art/text integration, variation, cultural relevance and preview clarity individually. Record observations rather than unexplained numerical beauty scores.
- Identify original text edition, commentary, modern retelling, art study, or historical manuscript explicitly. These categories are not interchangeable.
- Reject cover-only evidence for an interior-design card. Put inaccessible but promising books in an acquisition queue.
- Keep actual title, edition/ISBN, creator credits and source link on every real-book card. Do not relabel real books with invented style names.
- Before public gallery hosting, record the specific image license or permission; otherwise provide a source link. No publisher contact, purchases, licensing request or account creation has been made.
- Do not infer that a historical manuscript is Vedic in the strict textual sense. This pass still lacks a visually reviewed Vedic-accent edition and a strong modern Devanagari devotional edition.
